## Phase 1 — Information Shaping
**Lesson 4.6 — Information Operations**  
**Objectives:** 4.6-ie-dimensions, 4.6-disinformation

### Inject

Donovian state media and aligned social accounts have launched a coordinated narrative claiming that Gorgas is preparing ethnic violence against Zabzimeki civilians. The campaign distributes fabricated footage, recycled clips stripped of context, and amplified social media posts designed to shape international opinion against Gorgas. Coalition signals intelligence has detected coordinated hashtag activity originating from Donovian-linked accounts, reaching audiences in 14 countries within 90 minutes. Open-source researchers have geolocated several video clips to Donovian training grounds — not Gorgas territory. The intelligence cell is tasked to characterize the IO campaign: identify which dimensions of the Information Environment are being targeted, classify the type of information being used in each report, and clarify the intelligence officer's correct role in supporting the coalition's response.

### Evidence Cards

**Card E1-1 — State Broadcast: "Gorgas Army Moves on Civilians"**
Donovian state television claims Gorgas military is mobilizing against Zabzimeki civilians. Broadcast aired at 1423L. The segment uses inflammatory framing and unverified footage. Within two hours, the same broadcast was re-aired by three Donovian-aligned regional networks. An open-source researcher later identified the visual material as footage from a Donovian military exercise conducted in 2023 — geolocated to Donovia, not Gorgas. The broadcast was produced and distributed intentionally.

**Card E1-2 — Separatist Social Post: Viral Video**
A separatist-aligned social media account (~140,000 followers) posted footage of armed troops, claiming it shows events in Zabzimek. Video metadata shows geolocation tags were stripped before posting. A reverse image search located the same footage on a Donovian military forum dated March 2023. Based on account behavior and posting history, the account appears to have shared the content believing it was current and authentic — there is no evidence of deliberate intent to deceive on the part of this specific account.

**Card E1-3 — Leaked Gorgas Readiness Memo**
A genuine Gorgas Ministry of Defense memo was published by a separatist channel and framed as proof of imminent offensive operations. The memo has been verified as authentic. It describes standard defensive readiness procedures, not offensive planning. The separatist channel published it with deliberately misleading framing to imply aggressive Gorgas intent. The content itself is factually accurate — it is the context and framing that constitute the information operation.

**Card E1-4 — Gorgas Official Denial: Press Conference**
The Gorgas MoD held a press conference at 1600L denying all claims of offensive military operations. No independent external verification was cited. Estimated audience reach was approximately 12 percent of the original Donovian broadcast reach. The denial did not specifically address the leaked memo or geolocated footage, reducing its effectiveness.

**Card E1-5 — Hashtag Coordination Analysis: OSINT**
Three campaign hashtags appeared simultaneously in 14 countries within 90 minutes of the initial broadcast. Timing analysis shows a synchronized initial push from accounts with nearly identical creation dates, follower patterns, and posting cadence — inconsistent with organic spread. Target languages include English, German, French, and Georgian, indicating deliberate audience targeting across NATO member states and Gorgas itself.

## Phase 2 — Cyber Disruption
**Lesson 4.5 — Cyber Intelligence**  
**Objectives:** 4.5-cyber-actors

### Inject

As Donovia's IO campaign continues, Gorgas experiences a surge of coordinated cyber activity. Logistics contractors supporting border operations receive spearphishing emails spoofing the Gorgas Ministry of Defense. The border crossing management database goes offline for three hours following a destructive malware incident. Municipal government websites are defaced with political slogans and Donovian imagery. Separately, official Gorgas government social media accounts begin posting content that contradicts Gorgas's stated positions — assessed as compromised.

Coalition intelligence is tasked to assess: Is this criminal opportunism exploiting the crisis, or a coordinated state-directed operation? The analysis must characterize the threat actors, apply the Cyber Kill Chain to the phishing incident, and distinguish Cyber Threat vs Cyber-Enabled Threat.

### Evidence Cards

**Card E2-1 — Spearphishing: Logistics Contractor**
A spearphishing email spoofing the Gorgas Ministry of Defense was delivered to logistics contractors supporting border operations. The email contained a document with an embedded payload. On execution, custom malware — not commercially available — was installed and established a Command and Control (C2) channel to an external server. The malware remained undetected for approximately 6 weeks. Extended dwell time, custom tooling, and use of a spoofed government identity are indicators associated with nation-state actors.

**Card E2-2 — Municipal Website Defacement**
At least nine Gorgas municipal government websites were defaced within a 90-minute window. Content included pro-Donovian slogans, Zabzimek separatist imagery, and a message claiming Gorgas was oppressing its own people. Forensic review found the attacker used a publicly available hacking tool — consistent with hacktivist tradecraft. No persistent access tools were installed. The attacker deleted some access logs, suggesting basic operational security awareness despite low technical sophistication.

**Card E2-3 — Border Database Outage: Destructive Malware**
The Gorgas border crossing management database experienced a 3-hour outage after destructive malware corrupted primary and backup data. No ransom demand was made — the attacker's goal was destruction, not financial gain. The timing, occurring 18 hours after the IO campaign launched, is assessed as unlikely to be coincidental.

**Card E2-4 — Compromised Gorgas Social Media Accounts**
Three official Gorgas government social media accounts began posting content inconsistent with Gorgas's stated policy positions. Posts were deleted within hours. Gorgas authorities confirmed accounts had been accessed from outside the country. In this case, the network was used as a medium to deliver a false message to an audience — amplifying the Donovian IO narrative. The goal was cognitive effect, not network disruption.

**Card E2-5 — Attribution Analysis: Threat Actor Indicators**
Attribution in cyberspace is probabilistic. Analysts use TTPs, malware characteristics, and behavioral patterns to assess likely actor. Nation-state indicators include: custom or zero-day malware, extended dwell time (months, not days), objectives aligned with strategic goals rather than financial gain, and technical sophistication beyond hacktivist capability. The Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis maps four vertices — Adversary, Capability, Infrastructure, Victim — to link attacks to a common actor.

## Phase 3 — GEOINT and Border Activity
**Lesson 4.1 — Geospatial Intelligence**  
**Objectives:** 4.1-geoint-elements, 4.1-geoint-limits

### Inject

Coalition imagery assets and a commercial electro-optical satellite pass have detected significant military activity north of the Gorgas border. A motor pool 12 km inside Donovian territory shows approximately 40 wheeled vehicles — including fuel tankers — staged in a pattern consistent with pre-movement preparation. A 48-hour Wide-Area Motion Imagery (WAMI) collection window covered the nearest Donovian border town. Full Motion Video (FMV) assets tracked a vehicle convoy moving south along Route 7 for 12 continuous minutes before the collection platform repositioned. Moving Target Indicator (MTI) data flagged 15 vehicles transiting the Zabzimek corridor overnight.

The cell must analyze the imagery picture: identify which GEOINT product provides what type of coverage, determine which product best supports retrospective analysis of historical vehicle movement, and assess what the imagery can and cannot confirm — particularly regarding a warehouse showing a heat signature.

### Evidence Cards

**Card E3-1 — EO Satellite Image: Motor Pool, D-3**
Commercial electro-optical image (timestamp: D-3). Resolution sufficient to distinguish wheeled vehicle types and count fuel tankers. Approximately 40 vehicles observed, staged in columns consistent with movement preparation. Two fuel tankers visible on the eastern edge. This sensor depends on ambient light — nighttime collection is not possible with EO. This is a single-point snapshot: it shows what was present at that moment, not movement over time.

**Card E3-2 — WAMI Coverage: Border Town, 48-Hour Window**
WAMI collection window: 48 hours ending at 0600L today. Sensor covered an area approximately 10 km × 10 km — the full border town and surrounding road network. WAMI records all movement continuously across the wide area; analysts can rewind and replay any location within that area at any point during the 48-hour window. Vehicle movement from three days ago can be reconstructed. Five separate vehicle clusters were tracked from assembly areas to staging positions.

**Card E3-3 — FMV Clip: Route 7 Convoy, 12 Minutes**
Collection window: 12 minutes of continuous video on a specific convoy of 8 vehicles moving south along Route 7 toward the Gorgas border. The FMV sensor provided a focused, high-detail ("soda straw") view. Lead vehicle confirmed as a wheeled armored transport. After 12 minutes, the platform had to reposition. FMV provides excellent detail on the specific target but cannot simultaneously cover other areas or reconstruct movement before the sensor was cued.

**Card E3-4 — Moving Target Indicator (MTI): Zabzimek Corridor, Overnight**
MTI detected and flagged movement of 15 vehicles transiting a route between Donovia and the Zabzimek region overnight. MTI works by detecting and flagging moving objects — it can identify that vehicles are moving and plot their tracks across a wide area, but unlike FMV does not provide continuous detailed video of any single vehicle. Valuable for detecting movement at night or in poor visibility.

**Card E3-5 — Analyst Note: Northeast Warehouse, Heat Signature**
A thermal sensor detected a heat signature consistent with human or equipment activity inside a warehouse on the northeast edge of the motor pool complex. The heat signature indicates the building is occupied or has active equipment, but the sensor cannot see through the warehouse walls. GEOINT — regardless of sensor type — cannot penetrate solid structures to determine what is inside.

### Activity 1 of 4 — Matching (4 pts)
**Objective:** 4.1-geoint-elements  
**Instruction:** Match each GEOINT product to its defining characteristic.

| GEOINT Product | ✓ Correct Match |
|---|---|
| Full Motion Video (FMV) | Focused, continuous video of a specific target — "soda straw" view; best for tracking one target in detail |
| Moving Target Indicator (MTI) | Detects and tracks moving vehicles across a wide area; generates movement plots |
| Wide-Area Motion Imagery (WAMI) | Wide-area persistent coverage; all movement recorded continuously and can be rewound for forensic look-back |
| Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) | Emits its own radar energy; provides all-weather, day/night imaging regardless of cloud cover or darkness |

> **Why It Matters:** Knowing which product to request for which intelligence question is a core GEOINT tasking skill. Requesting FMV when you need historical look-back, or WAMI when you only need a focused 12-minute track, wastes collection time and leaves gaps.

### Activity 3 of 4 — Classification (3 pts)
**Objective:** 4.1-geoint-elements  
**Instruction:** Classify each sensor as Active or Passive. Active sensors emit their own energy. Passive sensors detect energy from an external source.

| Sensor | ✓ Correct Answer | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Electro-optical (EO) camera — captures reflected sunlight to produce imagery | **Passive** | EO waits for sunlight — it receives, not emits. |
| SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar) — emits radar pulses and records the return signal | **Active** | SAR sends out its own radar pulses — it emits. |
| FLIR thermal sensor — detects heat energy emitted by vehicles and people | **Passive** | Objects emit heat; the sensor only receives. FLIR does not emit. |

> **Why It Matters:** Active sensors like SAR are detectable because they emit energy adversaries can intercept or jam. Passive sensors emit nothing. Active sensors work in all weather and at night; passive EO sensors require ambient light.

### Activity 4 of 4 — Multi-Select (2 pts)
**Objective:** 4.1-geoint-limits  
**Instruction:** Select ALL intelligence questions that GEOINT imagery CANNOT answer about the northeast warehouse showing a heat signature.

| Option | ✓ Select? |
|---|---|

| Whether weapons or military equipment are stored inside the building | **✓ SELECT** — sensors cannot penetrate walls |

## Phase 4 — EMS, ELINT & Radar Kill Chain
**Lessons 4.2 & 4.3 — EM Theory & Radar**  
**Objectives:** 4.2-em-basics, 4.2-elint-vs-comint, 4.3-radar-kill-chain

### Inject

As Donovia's IO campaign and cyber disruptions continue, coalition SIGINT collectors begin detecting a surge of radar emissions along the Donovian side of the border. Emissions span multiple frequency bands — from long-range Early Warning (EW) pulses to a brief, alarming Fire Control (FC) spike that lasted only four seconds before ceasing. A Ground Control Intercept (GCI) voice intercept was also recorded, consistent with directing fighter aircraft toward a contact.

The current collector position is partially blocked by a ridgeline that interrupts line-of-sight to one of the primary emitter sites. An alternate hilltop position 18 km to the east has been identified with clear line-of-sight to all detected emitters.

The cell must: classify each emission as ELINT or COMINT, apply the kill chain framework to assess how close Donovia is to an engagement decision, and recommend the correct response to the terrain masking problem.

### Evidence Cards

**Card E4-1 — Early Warning Radar (EW): Long-Range Detection**
VHF-band radar emissions detected at approximately 380 km range. Long pulse, intermittent activation. Assessed as Early Warning radar. EW radar is the Find layer of the Integrated Air Defense System — its activation indicates Donovia is scanning for airborne threats at long range. Detection of EW emissions confirms the sensor is active but does not indicate imminent engagement. This is a non-communications electromagnetic emission (ELINT).

**Card E4-2 — Target Acquisition Radar (TA): Three Bursts Over Six Hours**
Three discrete emission bursts recorded from an L/S-band radar over a 6-hour window. L/S-band operates at higher frequency than VHF — shorter wavelength, shorter range, better resolution and precision. Consistent with a Target Acquisition (TA) radar that acquires and tracks specific contacts. TA radar activation indicates Donovia has moved from broad-area detection to tracking specific contacts — a significant escalation. ELINT.

**Card E4-3 — Fire Control Radar (FC) Spike: X-Band, 4 Seconds**
A 4-second emission burst detected in the X-band. X-band produces very short wavelengths enabling extremely fine angular resolution and precise range measurement: characteristics required for weapons guidance. A Fire Control (FC) radar lock-on, even briefly, indicates the IADS has moved to the Engage stage of the kill chain. FC radar activation is the most imminent indicator available in an emissions picture. ELINT.

**Card E4-4 — Ground Control Intercept (GCI) Voice Intercept: Encrypted, Aircraft Coordination**
An encrypted voice radio transmission was intercepted from a frequency and emitter location consistent with a GCI station. Transmission timing, frequency, and call patterns are consistent with a GCI controller directing fighter aircraft toward an airborne contact. GCI is the communications layer of the IADS. Unlike the other emissions, this is a communications intercept — not a radar emission. This determines its SIGINT category (COMINT).

**Card E4-5 — Terrain Masking: Ridgeline Blocks Line-of-Sight**
A ridgeline approximately 60 km from the current collector position creates a physical obstruction that blocks the direct line-of-sight path to the Donovian Early Warning radar site. Collection from this emitter is intermittent. Electromagnetic waves travel in straight lines — they cannot bend around terrain features. This is not a sensor malfunction; it is a fundamental physics constraint. No amount of signal processing can recover emissions blocked by terrain.

**Card E4-6 — Alternate Collector Position: Eastern Hilltop**
A hilltop 18 km to the east has been identified. Elevation and azimuth analysis confirms unobstructed line-of-sight from this hilltop to all four identified emitter sites: the EW radar, the TA radar, the FC radar, and the GCI station. Repositioning would resolve the terrain masking problem and improve collection continuity on all emitters.

### Activity 1 of 5 — Matching (3 pts)
**Objective:** 4.2-em-basics  
**Instruction:** Match each frequency characteristic to its operational effect.

| Frequency Characteristic | ✓ Correct Match |
|---|---|
| Low frequency / long wavelength | Long detection range, coarser resolution — suited for Early Warning (EW) at maximum distance |
| High frequency / short wavelength | Short range, fine resolution — suited for Fire Control (FC) precision targeting and weapons guidance |
| Mid-range frequency | Balance of range and resolution — suited for Target Acquisition (TA) and tracking |

> **Why It Matters:** The FC radar spike (Card 3) is X-band — highest frequency, which is what makes it capable of guiding a weapon to a target. The EW radar (Card 1) is VHF — low frequency, which is why it can detect targets at 380 km even though it cannot identify them precisely.

### Activity 2 of 5 — Classification (4 pts)
**Objective:** 4.2-elint-vs-comint  
**Instruction:** Classify each intercepted emission as ELINT or COMINT. ELINT = intelligence from non-communications electronic emitters (radars, beacons). COMINT = intelligence from voice and data communications.

| Item | ✓ Correct Answer |
|---|---|
| Early Warning radar pulse — a non-communications radar emission used for air surveillance | **ELINT** |
| Fire Control radar spike — a precision radar emission guiding a weapon system | **ELINT** |
| GCI encrypted voice transmission — a radio communication between controller and aircrew | **COMINT** |
| Logistics convoy radio check — voice communications between vehicles on a route | **COMINT** |

> **Why It Matters:** ELINT and COMINT are processed through different systems and chains. Misclassifying an emission means it goes to the wrong analyst team. The FC radar spike (ELINT) goes to an electronic warfare analyst. The GCI voice intercept (COMINT) goes to a linguist or signals analyst.

## Phase 5 — Infrared Threats 101
**Lesson 4.4 — IR Threats & Airframe Survival**  
**Objectives:** 4.4-ir-signatures

### Inject

As Donovia's border pressure increases, coalition aircrews supporting patrols through the Zabzimek corridor begin reporting more nighttime missile activity along the same route. Forward-Looking Infrared (FLIR) passes from a coalition aircraft reveal warm vehicle signatures on unimproved tracks, and a suspected IR-guided Man-Portable Air Defense System (MANPADS) position has been identified near the route. Analysts also note that one newer missile type appears to use an Imaging Infrared (IIR) seeker rather than a simple hot-spot tracker.

The cell must: identify the primary IR vulnerability on the aircraft, distinguish hot-spot seekers from IIR seekers, and choose the correct countermeasure for each threat type.

### Evidence Cards

**Card E5-1 — Hot Engine Exhaust: Primary Infrared (IR) Vulnerability**
IR seekers look for heat. The hottest, most exposed part of an aircraft is the engine exhaust — especially during high power settings and afterburner use. That bright heat source is the primary vulnerability for an IR-homing missile. Aspect angle matters: the closer the missile looks straight into the exhaust, the stronger the signature becomes.

**Card E5-2 — IR-Homing MANPADS: Hot-Spot Tracker**
A hot-spot tracker is the classic IR-homing missile seeker. It does not build a full image of the aircraft; it simply chases the hottest point. If a flare is hotter than the aircraft exhaust, the seeker can be pulled away from the airframe and into the decoy. This is why flares remain the standard countermeasure against basic IR-homing threats.

**Card E5-3 — Imaging Infrared (IIR) Seeker: Shape Recognition**
An Imaging Infrared seeker sees the whole thermal silhouette, not just a single bright dot. That means it can compare shape, contrast, and target patterning. A flare is still a bright point, but the aircraft remains a recognizable thermal object. IIR seekers are much harder to defeat with flares alone and typically require tactical maneuver in addition to decoy use.

**Card E5-4 — Forward-Looking Infrared (FLIR) Overflight: Surface Heat Detection**
FLIR is a thermal imaging sensor used by aircraft and drones to observe the ground in low light or at night. It detects heat signatures on the surface, making it ideal for spotting vehicles, exhaust plumes, and recent movement. FLIR is a passive sensor — it does not emit energy to collect the picture.

**Card E5-5 — Infrared Search and Track (IRST): Passive Fighter Sensor**
IRST is a passive infrared sensor used on fighter aircraft to detect and track other aircraft. Because it does not send out radar energy, it is harder to detect than an active radar system. Useful for finding hot targets at range and for supporting silent air-to-air search.

**Card E5-6 — Space-Based Infrared System (SBIRS): Missile Warning**
SBIRS is a space-based infrared system built to detect rocket motor exhaust and launch plumes. Its purpose is missile warning and strategic alerting. One of the best examples of a space-based IR sensor doing a specific warning mission.

**Card E7-1 — Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO): Persistent Theater Coverage**
GEO sits about 35,786 km above the equator and remains fixed relative to one spot on Earth. Ideal for persistent coverage — especially communications and missile warning. GEO is the classic "staring" orbit.

**Card E7-2 — Low Earth Orbit (LEO): High-Resolution Imaging**
LEO sits roughly 160 to 2,000 km above Earth. Close to the surface, so it supports high-resolution imagery and quick revisits. Tradeoff: any one satellite moves quickly across the area of interest, so persistence requires a constellation.

**Card E7-3 — Medium Earth Orbit (MEO): Navigation Constellation**
MEO sits between LEO and GEO, commonly used for navigation constellations such as GPS / NAVSTAR. MEO is the backbone of position, navigation, and timing (PNT) services that coalition forces depend on for maneuver and targeting.

**Card E7-4 — Polar Orbit: Global Coverage Including the Poles**
A polar orbit passes close to or over both poles as Earth rotates underneath. Gives broad global coverage, including Arctic and Antarctic regions that GEO cannot watch well. Useful for reconnaissance and weather missions that need worldwide reach.

**Card E7-6 — Kinetic Counterspace: Escalation Reference (No Indicators)**
Kinetic counterspace is the most escalatory category of space attack because it physically destroys the target spacecraft and can create debris that threatens other satellites in the same orbit. **Important for this scenario: no Donovian kinetic counterspace activity has been observed.** All space-domain interference to date is non-kinetic (Card 5). ## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Geospatial Intelligence (GEOINT) | Imagery, IMINT, and geospatial information used together. |
| Imagery Intelligence (IMINT) | Intelligence derived from interpretation of imagery. |
| Electro-Optical (EO) | Passive visible-light imaging. |
| Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) | Active radar imagery that works day and night in most weather. |
| Full Motion Video (FMV) | A focused moving video feed used for tracking and observation. |
| Wide-Area Motion Imagery (WAMI) | Broad-area motion coverage with forensic look-back capability. |
| Moving Target Indicator (MTI) | A sensor function that flags motion in a scene. |
| Low Earth Orbit (LEO) | About 160 to 2,000 km altitude; best for high-resolution imagery. |
| Medium Earth Orbit (MEO) | About 2,000 to 35,786 km; commonly used for GPS / navigation. |
| Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO) | About 35,786 km; fixed over one region for persistent coverage. |
| Highly Elliptical Orbit (HEO) | Used for highly inclined or polar coverage. |
| Electronic Intelligence (ELINT) | Intelligence from non-communications emitters such as radars, beacons, and jammers. |
| Communications Intelligence (COMINT) | Intelligence from voice and data communications. |
| Integrated Air Defense System (IADS) | A system of systems using multiple radars, command and control, and interceptors. |
| Early Warning (EW) | The long-range Find layer in an IADS. |
| Target Acquisition (TA) | A precision radar that transitions detection into tracking. |
| Fire Control (FC) | The precision guidance layer used to support weapons engagement. |
| Ground Control Intercept (GCI) | Radar and communications support that vectors interceptors onto a target. |
| Kill chain | Find - Fix - Track - Target - Engage - Assess; the sequence used to describe engagement progression. |
| Line of Sight (LOS) | The direct path required for most electromagnetic, optical, and IR collection. |
| Man-Portable Air Defense System (MANPADS) | Usually a shoulder-fired IR-guided missile system. |
| Infrared (IR) | The heat spectrum used by many sensors and seekers. |
| Forward-Looking Infrared (FLIR) | A passive thermal imaging sensor used from aircraft or drones. |
| Infrared Search and Track (IRST) | A passive fighter sensor used to detect and track aircraft heat signatures. |
| Space-Based Infrared System (SBIRS) | Satellite missile warning system that watches for launch plumes. |
| Imaging Infrared (IIR) | A seeker that builds a thermal picture of the target rather than chasing one hot point. |
| Hot-spot tracker | A simple IR seeker that locks onto the hottest point it can see. |
| Flares | IR countermeasure that creates a hotter decoy than the aircraft exhaust. |
| Chaff | Radar countermeasure made of reflective material used against radar-guided threats. |
| IRCM | Infrared Countermeasures — techniques or systems designed to defeat IR-guided weapons. |
| MISO | Military Information Support Operations — planned influence operations aimed at foreign audiences. |
| MILDEC | Military Deception — deception directed at adversary decision-makers. |
| Disinformation | Deliberately false information shared to deceive. |
| Misinformation | Inaccurate information shared without intent to deceive. |
| Physical dimension | The hardware and infrastructure layer of the information environment. |
| Informational dimension | The data, content, and signals layer of the information environment. |
| Cognitive dimension | The beliefs, perceptions, and decision-making layer of the information environment. |
| Cyber threat | A cyber attack that targets network integrity, availability, or confidentiality. |
| Cyber-enabled threat | A threat that uses cyberspace as a delivery medium to create another effect, such as influence. |
| Cyber kill chain | Reconnaissance, weaponization / delivery, exploitation / installation, command and control, actions on objectives. |
| C2 | Command and Control — the communications link used by an attacker or commander to direct actions. |
| TTP | Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures — the methods and patterns used by an actor. |
| Dwell time | The length of time an intrusion remains undetected inside a network or environment. |
| Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis | A cyber attribution framework that links adversary, capability, infrastructure, and victim across incidents. |
| PIR | Priority Intelligence Requirement — the commander's top intelligence question. |
| ISR | Intelligence, Surveillance, Reconnaissance — the broader collection and analysis cycle. |
| P&D | Planning and Direction — the ISR stage where requirements become collection tasks. |
| P&E | Processing and Exploitation — the ISR stage where raw data is turned into usable intelligence. |
| DCGS | Distributed Common Ground System — the primary processing and exploitation system in many ISR workflows. |
| PNT | Positioning, Navigation, and Timing — the core space-enabled navigation service. |
| SATCOM | Satellite Communications — communications relayed through space assets. |
| ASAT | Anti-Satellite weapon — a weapon designed to damage or destroy a satellite. |
| Counterspace | Actions to deny, degrade, disrupt, destroy, or deceive space capabilities. |
| Kinetic counterspace | Counterspace that physically destroys or damages a space system. |
| Non-kinetic counterspace | Counterspace that disrupts, jams, dazzles, or degrades without physical destruction. |
