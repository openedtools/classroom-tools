Scenario — The Bakery Dilemma A few days before Thanksgiving, a bakery producing specially prepared bread for several grocery chains begins losing batches — loaves coming out burned and worthless. Three separate batches overbaked across two crews, each using their own set of five gas ovens. A serviceman inspected the ovens and thermostats and found nothing wrong, noting that a gas pressure problem would affect all ovens, not just two. Three weeks prior, employees were denied additional benefits; two weeks prior, Michael was fired amid employee unrest. Frank (Crew 2) was seen meeting Michael in the parking lot the day before. That morning, a delayed flour shipment from a new supplier arrived — and only batches made with the new flour overbaked.

Key Takeaways
1
The primary objective of ACH is to minimize confirmation bias by identifying the hypothesis with the least inconsistent evidence, not the one with the most support.
2
A single piece of highly discriminating evidence can fundamentally alter an analytical conclusion, turning circumstantial indicators into irrelevant data points.
3
Evidence that relates exclusively to human emotion or motivation provides zero analytical value when evaluated against mechanical or material failures.
Evidence Rationale

⚠ Instructor Note — E10 / H1 Edge Case

A student may argue that E10 is Consistent with Sabotage by suggesting employees deliberately contaminated the new flour supply. While this demonstrates creative thinking, guide the student back to the available evidence: Frank (the prime suspect) successfully baked his first batch using the old flour. If a coordinated effort specifically targeted only the new flour, it requires a complex, multi-step assumption with no supporting evidence. In structured analysis, when a simpler explanation — defective supplier goods — carries zero inconsistencies and requires fewer assumptions, it is the mathematically and logically superior hypothesis.

▶ Facilitation Recommendation

Focus the majority of debrief time on Evidence Item 10. Before E10 is applied, Sabotage appears highly likely due to the volume of circumstantial human motive evidence (E5–E8). E10 demonstrates how a single piece of highly diagnostic evidence can completely invert the leading hypothesis by exposing a logical inconsistency — the core value of the ACH method.
